M8 Greyhound

light armored reconnaissance car · 1943–1945

What could a fast armored car, with a small gun and a thin floor, do in a war of tanks?

The M8 was a six-wheeled armored car, built by Ford, with a 37 mm gun in an open turret. In the US Army, it was the vehicle of the reconnaissance squadrons. Greyhound, after the racing dog, was the name the British gave it.

For Brazil, it has a place apart: it was the armored car of the 1st Reconnaissance Squadron of the Brazilian Expeditionary Force, the only Brazilian unit to use armor in the Second World War, according to the magazine of the Army's Armor Instruction Center.

On the board 1What it was for

The American manual of 1943 defines the purpose in one line: to give high-speed mobility, defensive fire and crew protection for reconnaissance. The M8 went ahead to see, come back and report. It was not a fighting piece.

Curiously, it was born for something else. The design began as a light wheeled tank destroyer, with the 37 mm gun. By the time it was ready, in 1942, that gun was no longer seen as an effective anti-tank weapon, and the vehicle was standardized as an armored reconnaissance car.

On the board 2Strengths

The road. At about 90 km/h on firm going, the M8 was much faster than any tank. The Lewis Army Museum sums it up: it ran well on a hard surface and badly on everything else.

The mechanics. The same museum describes it as mechanically sound, and Military Vehicles magazine, as generally more reliable than tracked vehicles of the same size. For a unit that lives far from the workshop, that counts.

On the board 3Weak points

The floor. With a quarter of an inch of plate underneath, the M8 was very vulnerable to mines, and the Germans used minefields on a large scale. The crews lined the floor with sandbags.

The open turret, which left the crew exposed to whatever came from above, and the gun. According to Steven Zaloga, as cited by the English Wikipedia, the 37 mm was not effective against the frontal armor of German tanks; it was good against light vehicles and infantry.

And the terrain. Off road, above all in mud and snow, mobility was poor, according to Nicholas Moran, as cited by the English Wikipedia, and worse than that of tracked and half-tracked vehicles, according to J. Rickard.

On the board 4The M8 in the FEB

The 1st Reconnaissance Squadron of the Brazilian division received the American equipment once it was in Italy. Each reconnaissance platoon had three patrols, and each patrol, one M8 and two jeeps. In the winter of 1944 to 1945, with the front stalled in the Apennines, the squadron was used practically as a rifle company; only from Montese on did it return to reconnaissance.

The two weak points on this page show up in the Brazilian record. According to Captain Plínio Pitaluga, the squadron commander, one M8 was put out of action by a German anti-tank mine during a reconnaissance, in the phase that runs from Montese to the Panaro river. On 28 April 1945, at Felegara, another was set on fire by a rocket-launcher projectile; the crew got out unharmed.

At Collecchio, on 26 and 27 April, it was the squadron that made the first contact with the vanguard of the German 148th Division, and the 37 mm gun answered the fire of enemy reconnaissance cars.

VerdictWhat the piece was worth on the board

On the board, the M8 is the piece that travels far and does not trade. Its worth lies in what it sees, not in what it destroys: getting there first, finding the enemy, measuring and coming back.

Used that way, it delivered. Put to fight, it was exposed at its weakest: the floor, the open turret, the small gun. The line attributed to Pitaluga by the English Wikipedia points the same way: the M8 is for reconnaissance, not for heavy combat.

MythsWhat is told and what the record shows

  • What is told

    Greyhound was the American name for the vehicle.

    What the record shows

    The name is British. The American manual of 1943 calls it simply the light armored car M8. The reason for the name differs: the speed, according to Military Vehicles magazine; the series of dog names the British were already giving to American armored cars, according to the English Wikipedia.

  • What is told

    11,000 or 12,000 of the M8 were built.

    What the record shows

    There were 8,523. The larger number adds in the M20, the turretless variant, with about 3,790.

  • What is told

    The Germans called the M8 cars Patton's ghosts, they were so quiet.

    What the record shows

    The story is repeated on many pages, always with no German document and no record from Patton's army. The best source found already gives it as an allegation.

  • What is told

    The M8 was a tank destroyer that did not work out.

    What the record shows

    It is a half-truth. The design began as a light tank destroyer, but the vehicle was already standardized in 1942 as an armored reconnaissance car, and that is what the manual defines it for.

QuestionsFrequently asked questions

How many M8 Greyhound cars were built?

8,523, by Ford, from March 1943 on. The totals of 11,000 or 12,000 add in the M20 variant, without a turret.

What gun did the M8 Greyhound carry?

A 37 mm M6 gun, in an open, hand-traversed turret, with 80 rounds. It was good against light vehicles and infantry, not against the front of German tanks.

Did the FEB use armor in Italy?

Yes: the M8, in the 1st Reconnaissance Squadron. There were 15 on establishment, with 13 in use, in the sources linked to the Army, and 13 in the Portuguese Wikipedia.

Why is the M8 called Greyhound?

The name was given by the British. The reason differs with the source: the vehicle's speed, or the series of dog names they gave to American armored cars.
