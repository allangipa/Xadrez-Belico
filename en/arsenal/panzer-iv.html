Panzer IV

medium tank · 1939–1945

How did a tank designed to support the infantry become the workhorse of the German armored divisions?

  • 8,553tanks built from 1937 to April 1945, at the Tank Museum and in Jentz and Doyle2+ sources
  • 211in service on 1 September 1939: about 7% of German tanks2+ sources
  • 80 mmof armor on the hull front of the H version, against 50 mm on the turret front in most sources2+ sources
  • 25 tin the H version, of 1943: the 1937 chassis close to its limit2+ sources
  • 38–42 km/htop speed on road, depending on the source and the versiondisputed

The Panzerkampfwagen IV began the war as a support piece: a short 75 mm gun to silence machine-gun nests and anti-tank guns, behind the tanks that would do the fighting against other tanks. In September 1939 there were 211, about 7% of German armor.

In 1942 it received a long gun and changed jobs. It was the only German tank built from the beginning to the end of the war, and in 1943, while the Panther was debuting by breaking down at Kursk, it was the one in the line. This page deals above all with the G and H versions, the long-barreled ones.

On the board 1What it was for: from support piece to battle tank

The Tank Museum describes the original Panzer IV as the close support tank of the armored regiments: the short 75 mm gun fired a high-explosive shell at low velocity, good against positions and poor against armor. Fighting tanks was the job of the Panzer III. In Poland, in September 1939, there were 211 Panzer IVs; according to J. Rickard, 76 were put out of action by 10 October, 19 of them total losses.

The encounter with the T-34, in 1941, reversed the roles. The Panzer IV chassis could take a bigger gun; the Panzer III chassis could not. In 1942 the Panzer IV received the long-barreled KwK 40, and, in Rickard's words, went from being a support tank to being the best battle tank in the German army. The American manual of 1945 records the same passage: it began the war with a short gun, as a support vehicle, and ended it as the standard medium tank.

On the board 2Strengths

The first is having been there the whole time. It was the only German tank in production from the start to the end of the war, which means spare parts, trained crews and workshops that knew it, on every front.

The second is the long gun. According to Thomas Jentz, as cited by the English Wikipedia, with it the Panzer IV could penetrate the T-34 at up to 1,200 meters, at any angle. A report from the 9th Panzer Division, cited by Tank Encyclopedia, says the T-34's armor was easily penetrated by the long 75 mm gun.

Against the Sherman, the comparison varies from historian to historian. For J. Rickard, in 1944 the Panzer IV was superior to the Sherman M4A2 at normal combat ranges. Max Hastings, as cited by the English Wikipedia, writes that the 75 mm Sherman had difficulty against the Panzer IV of the final versions.

On the board 3Weak points

The chassis dated from 1937 and could grow no further. According to Yuri Pasholok, the H version reached 25 tonnes, which forced a change to the final drive, and the turret front stayed at 50 mm because there was no weight margin. The J version, the last, lost the electric turret traverse and was left with the manual one only.

The sides had 30 mm, and the skirts that protected them from anti-tank rifles, according to Hastings, as cited by the English Wikipedia, were easily torn off in scrub and on rough ground.

And there was its place in the German plan. The American manual of 1945 treats the Panzer IV as a stopgap, kept until there were enough Panthers. The Panther never reached that number.

VerdictWhat the piece was worth on the board

On the board, the Panzer IV is the piece that was not designed for the role it ended up playing. It came in as support and, because its chassis could take a bigger gun, became the fighting piece when the opponent turned up with better armor.

The price of that adaptation was the limit. Each improvement, a longer gun, more armor at the front, skirts, used up the margin of a 1937 design, and by 1943 it was gone. Germany went on building it until April 1945 because the replacement did not come in quantity.

MythsWhat is told and what the record shows

  • What is told

    The Panzer IV's side skirts were made against the bazooka and the PIAT.

    What the record shows

    The German documents of 1943 gathered by the site Panzerworld speak of protection against anti-tank rifles, the Soviet 14.5 mm ones; Tank Encyclopedia says the same. The shaped-charge version exists in print: Bryan Perrett, as cited by the English Wikipedia, writes that the skirts could set off weapons such as the British PIAT prematurely. The sources disagree, with the documents of the period on the side of the anti-tank rifle.

  • What is told

    The Panzer IV was Germany's main tank from 1939.

    What the record shows

    On 1 September 1939 there were 211, about 7% of the German armored force, and the role was support, with a short gun. Only in 1942, with the long gun, did it take on the fight against tanks.

  • What is told

    The H version was the most produced, with 3,774 tanks.

    What the record shows

    That is one of the counts. Jentz and Doyle, as cited by Wikipedia, and Tank Encyclopedia give about 2,322 for the H, and in that count the most numerous is the J, with about 3,160.

  • What is told

    The F2 is a separate version.

    What the record shows

    F2 was the provisional name of the first long-barreled Panzer IVs, later renamed G. The date of the change differs: June 1942 in J. Rickard, July in Tank Encyclopedia, September in the German Wikipedia.

QuestionsFrequently asked questions

How many Panzer IVs were built?

8,553 tanks, from 1937 to April 1945, by the count of the Tank Museum and of Jentz and Doyle. The breakdown by version differs: the H appears with about 2,322 or with 3,774, depending on the author.

What gun did the Panzer IV carry?

A 75 mm gun in three forms: the short-barreled KwK 37 L/24, up to the F1 version; the KwK 40 L/43 from 1942 on; and the KwK 40 L/48 on the late G and on the H.

What were the Panzer IV's skirts for?

By the German documents of 1943, to protect the sides against the Soviet 14.5 mm anti-tank rifles. There is a version in print that ties them to shaped-charge weapons; the sources disagree.

Did the Panzer IV fight at Kursk?

Yes. At Prokhorovka, on 12 July 1943, the 4 tanks that II SS Panzer Corps lost for good were all Panzer IVs, according to Roman Töppel from the German archives.
