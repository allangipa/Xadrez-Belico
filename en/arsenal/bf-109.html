Messerschmitt Bf 109

single-seat fighter · 1937–1945

How did a fighter that climbed and dived better than its rivals run out of time to fight over England?

The Bf 109 was the Luftwaffe's fighter from the beginning to the end of the war. This page covers the E version, the Emil, in the E-3 and E-4 subtypes: the one that crossed the English Channel in 1940.

It was a small aircraft around a big engine. It climbed well, dived without its engine cutting out and carried 20 mm cannon when the British fighters had only machine guns. And it carried little fuel: in the Battle of Britain, the German escort fighter reached the target already counting the minutes to turn back.

On the board 1What it was for

The Bf 109 was born as an interception and air superiority fighter, and debuted in the Spanish Civil War with the Condor Legion. According to Jon Guttman, the first production Bf 109 B aircraft entered service in Spain in March 1937, and the first victory came on 6 April of that year, by Lieutenant Günther Lützow.

In 1940, over England, the role changed without the aircraft changing: from free hunter, it became bomber escort, far from its bases and tied to the bombers' speed. It was the only single-engine fighter the Luftwaffe had to take on the Spitfire and the Hurricane.

On the board 2Strengths

The RAF Museum sums it up in one sentence: the Bf 109 E climbed faster and had heavier armament than the Hurricane and the Spitfire. The 20 mm cannon fired a high-explosive shell; the British fighters of 1940 had eight rifle-caliber machine guns.

The direct-injection engine gave an advantage in maneuver. The British trial with a captured Emil lists among the good points that the engine does not cut out immediately under negative acceleration. In the carbureted Merlin, it did: Werner Mölders, after flying a captured Spitfire, noted that a sharp push on the stick made the engine cut out. A German pilot being chased could push the nose down and dive; a British pilot who tried to follow him the same way lost power for an instant.

On the board 3Weak points

The first is range, and that was what weighed in 1940. With about 660 km on the internal tank alone, the Emil left the Pas-de-Calais, crossed the Channel and had minutes over the target. The RAF Museum speaks of 20 minutes of flight over southeast England; the Smithsonian, of about 20 minutes over Britain, and adds that many pilots ran out of fuel and came down in the Channel. For London, farther away, Wagner and Nowarra, as cited by the English Wikipedia, give 10 minutes.

The second is the turn. In the British trial of 1940, at 12,000 feet, the minimum radius of the Bf 109 E was 885 feet, against 696 for the Spitfire, and the full turn took about 25 seconds against 19. The same trial records controls that were too heavy above 250 miles per hour and a cramped cockpit, in which the pilot could barely apply sideways force on the stick.

The third is the ground. The narrow-track landing gear, added to the engine's torque, made landing and takeoff harder than in the British fighters, according to the same trial. Walter Boyne, as cited by the English Wikipedia, writes that at least 10% of all Bf 109 fighters were lost in landing and takeoff accidents, 1,500 of them between 1939 and 1941.

BattlesWhich battles it fought in

  • Episode 07 · 10 July to 31 October 1940The Battle of Britain

    It was the Luftwaffe's escort fighter, and it did not have the range to stay over the target. On 15 August 1940, the air fleet from Norway attacked northern England without it: of 115 bombers and 35 fighters, 75 aircraft were destroyed. After the change of target to London, on 7 September, the journey grew longer, and over the capital the Bf 109 had about ten minutes of combat before turning back.

VerdictWhat the piece was worth on the board

On the board, the Bf 109 E is a strong piece with a short reach. In fighter-against-fighter encounters, the British record of the time treats it as a worthy rival: faster in the climb, better armed, worse in the turn.

What limited it in 1940 was the square it was placed on. A fighter designed to defend its own sky had to escort bombers on the far side of an arm of the sea, and the plan depended on its staying where the fuel would not let it. The drop tank that would have solved this only appeared on the E-7, when the battle was already at the end of August.

MythsWhat is told and what the record shows

  • What is told

    The right name is Me 109; Bf 109 is a mistake.

    What the record shows

    The official designation is Bf 109, from Bayerische Flugzeugwerke, where the design began in 1934. But Me 109 was in common use in Germany itself: it appears in the captions of the military photographers kept in the Bundesarchiv and on the plate of an E-4 captured in 1940.

  • What is told

    A third of all Bf 109 fighters were destroyed in landing and takeoff accidents.

    What the record shows

    The figure with a book behind it is much smaller: at least 10%, according to Walter Boyne, as cited by the English Wikipedia. For the one-third version no primary source was found. Neither of the two numbers was checked here against Luftwaffe loss records.

  • What is told

    The Bf 109 had exactly ten minutes of combat over England.

    What the record shows

    The 10 minutes refer to London and come from Wagner and Nowarra, as cited by the English Wikipedia. The RAF Museum and the Smithsonian write 20 minutes, over southeast England and over Britain. The two counts can coexist, because they speak of different distances; what does not exist is a single number.

  • What is told

    There was no drop tank for the Bf 109 in the Battle of Britain.

    What the record shows

    There was, at the end. The E-7, the first version to carry a tank of 300 liters, entered service at the end of August 1940 according to Francis Mason, as cited by the English Wikipedia, with 32 aircraft on strength on 31 August, against 339 of the E-4 and 375 of the E-1 on the same date.

QuestionsFrequently asked questions

What is the difference between Bf 109 and Me 109?

It is the same aircraft. Bf 109 is the official designation, from Bayerische Flugzeugwerke; Me 109 was the name in common use in Germany and among the British.

How many Bf 109 fighters were built?

Between 33,000 and 34,000, from 1936 to 1945. The counts differ: 33,984, 34,248 or about 33,300, depending on the source.

What was the Bf 109's armament in the Battle of Britain?

On the E-3 and the E-4, two MG 17 machine guns of 7.92 mm above the engine and two cannon of 20 mm, one in each wing. The E-1 carried four MG 17 machine guns.

How long could the Bf 109 fight over England?

The sources differ. About 10 minutes over London, according to Wagner and Nowarra; 20 minutes over southeast England, according to the RAF Museum and the Smithsonian.

Was the Bf 109 better than the Spitfire?

It depended on the maneuver. By the RAF Museum, the Bf 109 E climbed faster and was better armed; in the British trial of 1940, the Spitfire turned tighter, with a radius of 696 feet against 885.
